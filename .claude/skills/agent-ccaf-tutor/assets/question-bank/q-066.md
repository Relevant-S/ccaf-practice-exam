## Question

A research agent has been running for 3 hours, accumulating findings in a single `findings` variable that's been re-included in every model call. The variable is now 8K tokens of mixed structured snippets and prose paragraphs, accumulated across ~20 sub-investigations.

The user adds a follow-up question that requires comparing two specific findings buried in the middle of `findings`.

What's the best next step?

## Options

- **A)** Continue passing the full 8K-token `findings` blob unchanged, plus the new question; let the model identify and use the two relevant findings.
  - **Rationale:** "Lost in the middle" — models reliably process the beginning and end of long inputs but may omit content from the middle. The two relevant findings are in the middle, which is exactly the failure zone. Doing nothing here will produce a degraded answer.
- **B)** Compress `findings` into a 500-word prose summary covering the main points across all 20 sub-investigations; pass the summary plus the new question.
  - **Rationale:** Aggressive paraphrase. The two specific findings needed are exactly the kind of detail that gets averaged out into "the main points." Compression at this size loses the precision the question requires.
- **C)** Restructure `findings` from a free-form blob into a structured object keyed by topic, where each topic holds `{claim, evidence, sources, date}`. Pass only the two relevant topic entries plus the new question.
  - **Rationale:** Correct. Two moves at once: (1) restructure the prose into a shape that's easy to slice, and (2) prune to the two topics that matter for this question. The model gets exactly what it needs, in a form it can reason over precisely. Future questions over the same `findings` also benefit from the restructuring.
- **D)** Embed each chunk of `findings` and use vector retrieval to surface the two relevant chunks for the new question.
  - **Rationale:** Vector retrieval is heavy infrastructure for what is a pruning problem. It also throws away the natural structure (topic, claim, source) in favor of embedding similarity, which is worse at this task. An 8K-token blob is small enough that restructuring is both simpler and more accurate.
