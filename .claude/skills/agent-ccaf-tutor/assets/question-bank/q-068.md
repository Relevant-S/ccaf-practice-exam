## Question

A support agent handles long conversations — 40 to 70 minutes, often covering three or four separate issues for the same customer. To keep the context window under control, the implementation summarizes older turns as the conversation grows.

Complaints are coming in: the agent quotes the wrong refund amount, forgets a delivery date the customer stated early on, and once contradicted a promise it had made 30 minutes earlier. The summarization itself is working — it just produces things like "customer discussed a refund and a delivery concern."

What is the right fix?

## Options

- **A)** Summarize less aggressively — keep more recent turns verbatim and only compress turns older than 30 minutes.
  - **Rationale:** Buys time but does not fix the mechanism. The lost facts are precisely the oldest ones (a date stated early, an amount agreed near the start), so a recency-biased policy keeps exactly the wrong half. Any threshold eventually eats the fact that mattered, because the flaw is that facts sit inside the summarized stream at all.
- **B)** Move the transcript into a vector store and retrieve the relevant passages on each turn instead of carrying history in the prompt.
  - **Rationale:** Wrong tool for this problem. Retrieval infrastructure earns its cost for large, persistent, cross-session knowledge — not for a single 70-minute conversation that fits with modest pruning. It also adds retrieval misses on the exact facts that must be certain, and drops the live thread the agent needs to stay consistent with its own promises.
- **C)** Start a fresh session for each distinct issue, carrying a written summary of the previous issue into the new one.
  - **Rationale:** The issues belong to one customer and interact — a refund promise made while discussing issue one constrains what can be said about issue three. Splitting sessions throws away the live conversation and brings back the same lossy-summary problem at every boundary. Starting fresh with a summary suits stale tool results, not a coherent conversation still in progress.
- **D)** Extract transactional facts — amounts, dates, order numbers, statuses, commitments made — into a persistent "case facts" block that is included in every prompt, kept outside the summarized history.
  - **Rationale:** Correct. It separates the two kinds of context by what they cost to lose. Narrative flow tolerates compression; hard facts do not, so they are pulled out of the compressible stream and re-sent verbatim every turn. The exam guide (T5.1) names this directly: a case-facts block outside summarized history. The summarizer keeps working on prose, and no threshold tuning is needed.
