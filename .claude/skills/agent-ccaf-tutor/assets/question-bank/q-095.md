## Question

An extraction pipeline has run with 100% human review for two months. The team now wants to auto-approve high-confidence extractions and keep monitoring quality afterwards.

Which TWO practices are most appropriate? (Choose 2.)

## Options

- **A)** Send a random sample of all extractions to reviewers instead of routing low-confidence items to them.
  - **Rationale:** Random sampling is for measuring quality. It is the wrong tool for deciding which items scarce reviewers should look at — low-confidence items would slip through unreviewed.
- **B)** After automating, keep a stratified random sample of the high-confidence extractions under review to measure error rates and catch new error patterns.
  - **Rationale:** Correct. Once items skip review, a stratified sample is how you keep measuring the error rate of the automated stream and spot new kinds of errors early.
- **C)** Use the model's self-reported 1–10 confidence, without calibration, as the auto-approval threshold.
  - **Rationale:** Raw self-reported confidence is poorly calibrated. Confidence scores must be calibrated against a labelled validation set before a threshold means anything.
- **D)** Stop all review of high-confidence extractions once overall accuracy passes 95%.
  - **Rationale:** With no review at all, nothing catches drift or new error patterns. And an overall figure can still hide a segment that is failing.
- **E)** Before automating, check accuracy by document type and by field, not just the overall number.
  - **Rationale:** Correct. An aggregate figure such as 97% can hide one document type or field that performs badly. Checking each segment first makes sure automation is safe everywhere.
