## Question

A customer raises three separate issues during one session: a refund inquiry (turns 1-15), a subscription question (turns 16-30), and a payment method update (turns 31-45). At turn 48, the customer asks "What happened with my refund?" The conversation is approaching context limits.
What strategy best maintains the agent's ability to address all issues throughout the session?

## Options

- **A)** Rely on MCP tools to re-fetch relevant information on demand when the customer references earlier issues.
  - **Rationale:** Re-fetching is fine for freshness, but it doesn't solve the context-length problem — and you lose what was said between the customer and the agent.
- **B)** Implement sliding window context that retains the most recent 30 turns.
  - **Rationale:** A pure sliding window silently drops the refund issue (turns 1-15) from context, which is exactly what the customer is asking about.
- **C)** Summarize earlier turns into a narrative description, preserving full message history only for the active issue.
  - **Rationale:** Narrative summaries are where amounts, dates and order numbers get lost. The exam guide (T5.1) names this as the risk of progressive summarization. The refund details the customer is asking about would only survive as a vague summary.
- **D)** Extract and persist structured issue data (order IDs, amounts, statuses) into a separate context layer.
  - **Rationale:** Correct. The exam guide (T5.1) lists this skill almost word for word: extract and persist structured issue data into a separate context layer for multi-issue sessions. The refund's facts stay exact, even when older turns are summarized or dropped.
